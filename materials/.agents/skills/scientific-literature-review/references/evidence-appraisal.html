Evidence appraisal

Choose an appraisal framework that matches the design. Do not collapse heterogeneous studies into one generic “quality score.”

Questions for every study

  • Is the research question and analysis population/model clearly defined?
  • Are experimental units and biological replicates correctly identified?
  • Are controls, randomization, blocking, blinding, exclusions, and missing data handled transparently?
  • Is the measurement valid for the claimed construct?
  • Are effect sizes and uncertainty reported?
  • Are multiple testing, model assumptions, batch effects, and confounding addressed?
  • Are methods, data, and code sufficient for reproduction?
  • Do conclusions stay within the design's causal limits?
  • Are funding and conflicts disclosed?

Design-specific routing

  • Randomized clinical studies: use a current trial risk-of-bias framework.
  • Observational studies: assess selection, confounding, measurement, missingness, and reporting.
  • Diagnostic studies: assess patient selection, index test, reference standard, flow, and timing.
  • Animal/in-vitro experiments: assess allocation, blinding, biological replication, dose/time design, and external validity.
  • Omics/ML studies: assess leakage, preprocessing fitted outside folds, independent validation, batch/site confounding, class imbalance, and calibration.
  • Systematic reviews: assess protocol registration, search completeness, duplicate screening, appraisal, synthesis, and reporting bias.

Synthesis labels

Use explicit labels such as:

  • replicated and directly applicable;
  • consistent but indirect;
  • mixed/heterogeneous;
  • single-study or exploratory;
  • preprint-only;
  • contradicted;
  • insufficient evidence.

Explain why the label applies. Never turn the label into a clinical recommendation without an appropriate authoritative guideline and qualified human review.

Clinical interpretation safeguards

  • Compare groups using the appropriate between-group effect and confidence interval; a significant pre/post change in one group and a non-significant change in another do not establish superiority.
  • Use AMSTAR 2 only for healthcare intervention reviews and judge critical domains, not a summed score. Other review types need compatible design-specific methods. See AMSTAR 2.
  • Keep reporting completeness, risk of bias, clinical importance, certainty and applicability separate. Individual-change MIC/MCID is not automatically a between-group threshold.
